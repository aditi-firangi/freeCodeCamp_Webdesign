Dr. Norman Borlaug

Here's a timeline of Dr. Borlaug's life:

  • 1914 - Born in Cresco, Iowa
  • 1933 - Leaves the family farm to attend the University of Minnesota, helped by a Depression-era program.
  • 1935 - Pauses school to earn money, working with the Civilian Conservation Corps helping people who were going hungry.
  • 1937 - Graduates from university and joins the US Forestry Service.
  • 1938 - Marries Margaret Gibson and returns to graduate school to study plant pathology under Elvin Charles Stakman.
  • 1941 - Tries to enlist after Pearl Harbor but is rejected; instead contributes to wartime applied-science projects.
  • 1942 - Earns a Ph.D. in Genetics and Plant Pathology.
  • 1944 - Turns down a lucrative offer to move to Mexico and lead a new plant pathology program, eventually breeding thousands of disease-resistant wheat strains.
  • 1945 - Develops a method allowing wheat to be grown twice a year, doubling yields.
  • 1953 - Crosses a short, sturdy dwarf wheat with a high-yielding American variety, creating a strain that thrives with fertilizer.
  • 1962 - Brings his high-yield wheat strains to India to help avert widespread famine.
  • 1970 - Awarded the Nobel Peace Prize.
  • 1983 - Helps several African nations dramatically increase their maize and sorghum harvests.
  • 1984 - Becomes a distinguished professor at Texas A&M University.
  • 2005 - Warns that the world's food supply will need to double by 2050 and argues for the responsible use of genetically modified crops.
  • 2009 - Passes away at the age of 95.
Indian Prime Minister Manmohan Singh honored Borlaug's intellect and lifelong dedication to peace and human progress.
— Manmohan Singh, 2009
